Checks cover exact starting/destination schema metadata, intended logical IDs, Unicode/nondefault names, both relationship directions, counts, optional-field absence, and unchanged pristine source fixtures. Producers terminate before their entire store directories are copied. Migration/reopen processes do not reseed data.

The negative control opens and migrates successfully, preserves both records and their relationships, but replaces both renamed values with empty defaults. The probe requires that exact corruption to be observed and detected. A random crash or compilation failure cannot satisfy the negative control.

Two independent review runs found no substantive defect in the synthetic pass claims. The reviewer confirmed stored schema metadata and tested output-directory reuse and a missing source store. Subsequent tightening made every prior-version assertion explicit; the final revised probe was rerun successfully and then verified from an installed copy. The reviewer did not separately rerun that final tightening.

During development, an inferred self-relationship produced an unintended seed graph. Explicit inverses and independent expected-seed assertions corrected it. This illustrates why comparing a migrated snapshot only against whatever a seed happened to produce is insufficient.

Scope: these are synthetic schemas generated by the current SDK. They do not prove compatibility with shipped historical app stores, every iOS release, CloudKit, custom migration stages, uniqueness-conflict policies, or production-scale data. This is stronger executable tooling and verification, not a comparative agent-superiority claim. The macOS CI job is configured but has not run remotely.
